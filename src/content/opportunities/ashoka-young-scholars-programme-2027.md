## At a glance

- **What it is:** the Young Scholars Programme (YSP), a short residential summer programme at Ashoka University, a private university in Sonipat, Haryana, near Delhi. "Residential" means students stay on campus.
- **Who can apply:** students entering Grade 9, 10, 11 or 12 in the 2027–28 school year, and students who have finished Grade 12 but have not yet started college. Good spoken and written English is needed.
- **When:** two editions, May 2027 (Edition 1) or June 2027 (Edition 2). The exact dates are not on the page yet.
- **Length:** 5 days or 10 days, depending on the programme.
- **Applications open:** 1 December 2026.
- **Scholarship deadline:** 14 March 2027. After this date you cannot apply for a scholarship.
- **Cost:** about ₹49,500 (5-day) or ₹85,000 (10-day), plus GST, for 2027. The application fee is ₹1,000, not refundable.

| Applied Sciences, Innovation & Research | 10 days | about ₹85,000 |
| Artificial Intelligence, Robotics & Computing | 10 days | about ₹85,000 |

The 10-day fee includes an optional stay on campus over the weekend in the middle. You may apply to more than one programme if the dates do not overlap.

## How to apply

1. From 1 December 2026, fill in the online application on Ashoka's website and pay the ₹1,000 fee.
2. Upload a scanned, signed final marksheet for 2025–26.
3. Write a 250-word essay. Ashoka checks essays for copying and for AI-written text, so write it yourself.
4. List up to three important activities outside class. Add certificates for anything at inter-school level or higher, and for leadership roles.
5. If you need help with the fee, apply for the merit-cum-need scholarship inside the same application, before 14 March 2027.

Ashoka reviews applications as they arrive and emails a decision within about two weeks. If your child is offered a place, the programme fee must be paid within 5 days of the offer.

## Is it worth it?

A short programme like this lets a student try a subject, live away from home for a few days and see what a university feels like. It is a summer course, separate from applying to Ashoka's degree programmes. For many families it is a big expense for a week or so, so think about what your child wants to learn, and look at free options too.
