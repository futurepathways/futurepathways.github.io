## At a glance

- **What it is:** a national challenge on MyGov, the Government of India's website where citizens take part in government activities. It asks students to share stories, local knowledge and ideas about **drinking water**. ("Peyjal" means drinking water and "gatha" means story.)
- **Who can enter:** students in Classes 8 to 12 across India.
- **Cost:** free. You need a MyGov account to submit.
- **Deadline:** 11:45 PM IST on 8 December 2026.
- **Reward:** shortlisted students get a **Certificate of National Recognition**. The page does not mention cash prizes.

## Who can enter

The MyGov page says the challenge is open to students in Classes 8 to 12 at schools recognised by State or Union Territory boards, CBSE, KVS (Kendriya Vidyalayas), NVS (Navodaya Vidyalayas), NIOS (the National Institute of Open Schooling), and "other eligible school systems across India".

**A note for CISCE, Cambridge and IB students:** the page does not name these boards and does not say which "other school systems" count. The challenge is not closed to them, but it is not clearly open either. The terms document linked from the MyGov page could not be opened when I checked. Before spending time on an entry, ask your school to check with MyGov, or read the terms on the task page if the link works for you.

Students are judged in two groups, so younger students do not compete with older ones:

- **Group A:** Classes 8 to 10, judged with age-appropriate expectations.
- **Group B:** Classes 11 and 12, judged with more weight on analysis and whether the idea would really work.

## The four categories

1. **My Drinking Water Story:** a true story about drinking water in your family, school or community. At least 40 to 50% of it should be directly about drinking water.
2. **Water Wisdom of My Village:** traditional knowledge, practices or structures for water, such as old wells, tanks or ways of storing rain. At least half should link this to drinking water. "Then and now" photos are welcome.
3. **Young Water Innovator: Finding an AQUA Solution:** a practical, low-cost idea to save, reuse, monitor or manage water. At least 40% should deal with drinking water or the systems behind it, and explain what difference it would make.
4. **My Local Water Body and Its Life:** a record of a lake, pond, tank, river or well near you. At least half should explain why it matters for drinking water or for keeping the source alive.

Tamil Nadu has a long history of temple tanks, eris (village irrigation tanks) and ooranis (drinking-water ponds), so students here have plenty to write about in categories 2 and 4.

## How entries are judged

| What the judges look for | Weight |
|---|---|
| Originality and authenticity (is it real and your own?) | 25% |
| Relevance to drinking water | 30% |
| Quality of the story or idea | 20% |
| Value of the local knowledge | 15% |
| Ethical documentation | 10% |

"Ethical documentation" means taking care when you record people and places: ask before you photograph or quote someone, show people with dignity, and keep everyone safe.

## How to enter

1. Open the Peyjal Gatha page on MyGov and log in (or register).
2. Pick one category and plan your entry around drinking water.
3. Talk to family, neighbours or elders, visit the water body, and take your own photos with permission.
4. Submit on the MyGov page before 8 December 2026.

The MyGov page does not list file types, word limits or languages. Check the terms on the task page before you submit.

**Note:** the MyGov page calls this "a proposed national competition", and it does not name the ministry or department that runs it. Entries are already open on the page. I will update this post if the details change.
