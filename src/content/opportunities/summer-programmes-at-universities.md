## What it is

A summer programme is a short course, usually 1 to 6 weeks, run by a university for school students during the summer holidays. You attend classes, do projects, sometimes live in a hostel on campus, and meet students from other countries.

There are three broad kinds:

| Kind | Examples (to be confirmed each year) | Typical cost (approximate) |
|---|---|---|
| Selective and free or funded | Research Science Institute (MIT), Yale Young Global Scholars (fee, with financial aid) | Free to approximately ₹5 lakh including travel |
| University-run, paid | Stanford, Oxford, Cambridge, LSE, Harvard and many others | Approximately ₹3 to ₹10 lakh including travel |
| Online | Many of the same universities, plus Coursera and edX courses | Free to approximately ₹2 lakh |

## Who can apply

- Usually Grades 10 to 12 (ages 15 to 18). A few accept Grades 8 and 9.
- Good school marks and, for selective programmes, essays, teacher recommendations and sometimes test scores.
- Many need a passport, a visa and a parent's consent. Start the passport early if you do not have one.

## What you will do or gain

- A taste of a subject at university level before you commit to it
- A project or piece of writing you can show later (useful for design, research and study-abroad applications)
- Confidence living away from home
- Friends and teachers from other countries

**Be honest about what it will not do.** Paying for a summer programme at a famous university does **not** improve your chance of admission to that university. Admission offices say this openly. Selective, free programmes like RSI are a different matter: being chosen is itself an achievement.

## How to apply, step by step

1. **September to November:** shortlist 3 to 5 programmes. Check age, dates and cost on the official site, not on an agent's site.
2. **November to January:** write the essays. Ask a teacher for a recommendation early. Collect your Grade 9 and 10 marks.
3. **December to March:** submit. Many selective programmes close in January; paid ones often stay open until April or until full.
4. **March to May:** if accepted, pay the deposit, apply for a visa, book travel and insurance.
5. **After the programme:** write down what you learned while it is fresh. It will help your applications later.

Your FP counsellor can read your essays and help you compare options. Ask early; counsellors are busiest in January and February.

## Cost and any scholarship

- Always check the fee page on the official site. Prices change each year.
- Add flights, visa fees, insurance and spending money to the headline fee.
- Many paid programmes offer need-based fee reductions. You usually have to ask, with proof of family income.
- Fully funded options for Indian students are rare but real. Also look at Indian options: summer research fellowships at IISc and the Indian Academy of Sciences, and summer schools at IISERs and IITs, which are far cheaper.

## Tips from the FP team

- **A cheaper programme with a real project beats an expensive one with only lectures.**
- Online programmes are worth it if they end with something you made.
- If money is tight, spend it on a good online course and a local internship instead. Universities abroad value what you did, not where you flew to.
- Never pay an agent who promises "guaranteed admission" through a summer programme. That is not how admission works.
- Keep every certificate and photo. You will need them for portfolios and applications.
