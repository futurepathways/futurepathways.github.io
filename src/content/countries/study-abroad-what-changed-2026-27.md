A short, plain update on the study-abroad rules that moved this year.

Every few months a family tells me something they heard about a visa or a fee, and half the time it is old news or a rumour. So I sat down and checked the official government and university pages myself. Below are the changes I could confirm, with the date each one starts and a link to the page that says so. Rupee figures are rough, at the exchange rates of 15 November 2026.

## How long is the UK Graduate visa now?

From 1 January 2027, it drops from 2 years to 18 months for new applications.

The **Graduate visa** (the UK's post-study work visa: it lets a student stay and work after finishing a degree) is getting shorter.

- If you apply **on or before 31 December 2026**, you can stay for **2 years**.
- If you apply **on or after 1 January 2027**, you can stay for **18 months**.
- A PhD graduate still gets **3 years**.

What it means for you: a student finishing Class 12 now will graduate in 2030 or later, so plan on **18 months**, not two years. The visa fee on the gov.uk page is **£937** (about ₹1.2 lakh), and there is also a yearly health charge, usually **£1,035 a year** (about ₹1.3 lakh a year) for this visa.

Read more: [UCL](/universities/ucl/), [Oxford](/universities/university-of-oxford/) and [Imperial](/universities/imperial-college-london/) spotlights, and my post on [UCAS personal statements](/countries/ucas-personal-statements/).

## Australia: both student visas cost more from 1 July 2026

Australia raised two fees on **1 July 2026**.

- **Student visa (subclass 500):** the main applicant now pays **AUD 2,500** (about ₹1.6 lakh), up from AUD 2,000.
- **Temporary Graduate visa (subclass 485):** this is Australia's work visa after a degree. The main applicant now pays **AUD 5,750** (about ₹3.7 lakh), up from AUD 4,600.

Together that is about **AUD 8,250**, or roughly **₹5.3 lakh**, in government fees alone, before health cover and living costs. For the degree route of the 485 visa, the applicant must be 35 or younger, and the stay is usually 2 to 3 years depending on the qualification.

Read more: the [University of Melbourne spotlight](/universities/university-of-melbourne/) and [how Australia and Canada make students job ready](/countries/from-study-to-career-australia-canada/).

## How many A Levels do Singapore universities want?

NUS and NTU both ask for at least four A Level subjects.

This is not brand new, but it catches many Cambridge families in India by surprise, because many students here take three A Levels.

- **NUS** (National University of Singapore) asks international A Level applicants for a good pass in **at least four A Level subjects**. AS Levels do not count towards this.
- **NTU** (Nanyang Technological University) also asks for **at least four A Level subjects in the same exam sitting**. A student with only three can still apply, and NTU looks at each case. But NTU says this does **not** apply to international students applying on a **subsidised tuition fee** basis.

Why that matters: the subsidised fee in Singapore comes from the **MOE Tuition Grant** (a large fee cut from Singapore's Ministry of Education). In return, an international student must work in Singapore for **3 years** after graduating. So at NTU, a student with three A Levels would usually be looking at the full, unsubsidised fee, which is far higher.

What to do: if Singapore is on your list, plan for a fourth A Level early, by Grade 10 or the start of Grade 11. NTU's window for Indian Standard 12 and UK A Level students is **15 October 2026 to 19 March 2027**. NUS takes international applications from **16 December 2026 to 17 February 2027**.

Read more: the [NUS spotlight](/universities/nus/), with fees with and without the grant.

## Malaysia: the Graduate Pass for Indian students has an end date

Malaysia's **Graduate Pass** lets a student who finishes a bachelor's degree or higher in Malaysia stay for **12 months** after graduating and work part-time in permitted jobs. Indian nationals were added to the list in 2024.

The official page says the arrangement for **Indian and Chinese nationals is valid until 31 December 2026**. Each case is decided one by one, and the student needs a **letter of good conduct** from the university or the embassy. The Malaysian Immigration Department lists the fee as **RM 600** (about ₹14,000) for the applicant.

What to do: if your child is graduating in Malaysia soon, check the official page before relying on this pass. I could not find any notice yet that it will run past 2026.

## Is there a new US fee on OPT?

Not yet: it is only a proposed rule.

**OPT** (Optional Practical Training) lets international students in the US work in their field after their degree. On **8 October 2026**, the US Department of Homeland Security published a **proposed rule** that would add new fees for each F-1 student on OPT, paid through the student's university:

- **US$70,000** for the first OPT period (about ₹66 lakh), and
- **US$30,000** for any later OPT (about ₹28 lakh).

Please read this carefully: it is a **proposal, not a final rule**. Public comments closed on 9 November 2026. The government can change it, drop it or make it final later, and nothing has changed yet for students. If it does become final, universities may decide which students they will pay for, so it could affect who gets OPT at all. I will update this page when there is news.

Read more: my post on [the rupee, the visa and the rethink](/countries/the-rupee-the-visa-and-the-rethink/).

## What I am telling families

- Cost the visas, not just the tuition. In Australia alone, the two visas now add about ₹5.3 lakh.
- Plan on the shorter work periods: 18 months in the UK from 2027.
- For Singapore, decide on a fourth A Level early.
- Treat the US OPT fee as "watch this space", not as a decision-maker yet.
- Check the official page yourself before paying anything. Rules are changing faster than they used to.
