## Why students choose this country

- **Shorter degrees.** Most bachelor's degrees take 3 years (4 in Scotland), so you finish a year earlier than in the USA or India.
- **Cambridge A Levels fit perfectly.** UK universities are designed around A Levels, so TIPS students apply with qualifications admissions teams know well.
- **One application for everything.** UCAS lets you apply to up to five courses with one form and one personal essay.
- **Deep subjects from day one.** You study your chosen subject from the first year, with few general courses.
- **Work after study.** The Graduate visa (check current rules) lets most graduates stay and work for a period after finishing.

## Popular courses

Engineering, computer science, business and management, economics, law (LLB), medicine (very competitive), psychology, architecture, design, and the liberal arts style "joint honours" degrees (for example Politics and Economics).

## Approximate cost of study and living

All figures are approximate and change every year. Check each university's fee page.

| Item | Approximate yearly cost |
|---|---|
| Tuition (most subjects) | £18,000 to £30,000 (approximately ₹19 to ₹32 lakh) |
| Tuition (medicine, some engineering, Oxford and Cambridge) | £35,000 to £55,000 |
| Living costs outside London | £10,000 to £13,000 |
| Living costs in London | £13,000 to £16,000 |

For the visa you must show money for the first year's fees plus a fixed living amount (set by the UK government; check GOV.UK for the current figure) held in the bank for 28 days.

## Tests needed

- **English:** IELTS Academic is the most accepted (approximately 6.0 to 7.0 overall depending on course). Many universities also accept TOEFL, PTE, or an IGCSE First Language English grade. Ask each university.
- **Subject tests:** only for some courses. UCAT for medicine and dentistry, LNAT for law at some universities, TMUA or MAT or STEP for maths at a few, BMAT has been withdrawn (check current rules).
- **Oxford and Cambridge:** extra admissions tests and interviews.
- **A Level predicted grades** are the heart of the application. Universities make "conditional offers" based on them.

## Application timeline

Approximate, for entry in September of your Grade 12 year plus one.

| When | What |
|---|---|
| Grade 11, April to August | Shortlist courses. Attend virtual open days. Start the personal statement. |
| Grade 12, September | School sends predicted grades. Register on UCAS. |
| Grade 12, mid October | **Deadline** for Oxford, Cambridge, medicine, dentistry, veterinary science |
| Grade 12, late January | **Main UCAS deadline** for everything else |
| February to May | Offers arrive. Reply by the UCAS deadline (usually early June). |
| August | A Level results. If you meet your offer, your place is confirmed. |
| June to August | Apply for the visa (up to 6 months before the course starts). |

Check the exact dates on UCAS each year; they shift slightly.

## Visa basics

You need a **Student visa**. In short: an unconditional offer and a CAS number from the university, proof of money, an English qualification, a TB test certificate (required for Indian applicants), and the visa fee plus the health surcharge. Apply online on GOV.UK, then give your fingerprints at a visa centre in India. Always use the official GOV.UK site, never an agent's version of it.

## Scholarships

- **GREAT Scholarships** (British Council and partner universities, for Indian students, approximately £10,000 each)
- **University scholarships:** most universities give automatic or application-based international scholarships of £2,000 to £10,000 a year. Look on each university's "international scholarships" page.
- **Commonwealth and Chevening** are for postgraduate study, not bachelor's.
- Full scholarships for bachelor's degrees are rare. Plan assuming you will pay most of the cost.

## Working while studying and after

- Student visa holders can usually work up to 20 hours a week during term and full time in holidays. Check your visa conditions.
- After finishing, the Graduate visa (currently 2 years for bachelor's and master's graduates; check the current rules, as they change) lets you work in the UK without a sponsor.

## Things to think about

- **Weather:** cold, grey winters with short days. Some students find this hard.
- **Distance:** about 10 hours by air from Chennai. Flights home are expensive at Christmas.
- **Safety:** UK campuses are generally safe. Universities have good student support services.
- **Food:** Indian food is easy to find in most cities.
- **Cost rises:** fees go up a little each year, and the pound moves against the rupee. Keep a buffer.
