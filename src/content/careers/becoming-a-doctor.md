## What is this career, in simple words

A doctor finds out what is wrong with a person and helps them get better. Some doctors treat everyday illnesses, some do surgery, some work only with children or with the heart or the brain, and some do research instead of seeing patients.

The first step to becoming a doctor in India is a degree called **MBBS** (Bachelor of Medicine, Bachelor of Surgery). After MBBS, many doctors study further to become specialists.

## A day in the life

A junior doctor in a hospital might:

- Start early with a "round", checking on every patient in the ward
- See new patients in the out-patient clinic, asking questions and examining them
- Order tests, read reports and decide on treatment
- Explain things clearly to patients and their families
- Write notes carefully, because other doctors depend on them
- Work long shifts, including nights and weekends

The work is tiring but very meaningful. Doctors often say the best part is seeing someone walk out healthy.

## Is it a good fit for you?

You might enjoy medicine if you:

- Like biology and are curious about how the body works
- Stay calm when things get stressful
- Are patient with people and good at listening
- Do not mind a long study path (at least 5.5 years before you can practise, often 8 to 10 years to become a specialist)
- Are comfortable with blood, illness and sometimes sad situations

If you like science but are not sure about patients, look at careers in research, biotechnology, pharmacy or public health too.

## What to study in school

- **Grades 9 and 10:** do well in Science and Maths. Build strong reading and study habits.
- **Grades 11 and 12:** Physics, Chemistry and Biology are compulsory for MBBS in India. English is also required. Maths is optional but keeps engineering and other doors open.
- Cambridge students: AS and A Level Biology, Chemistry and Physics are accepted, but you must still write NEET for Indian colleges. Check the latest NEET eligibility rules on the official site.

## Courses that lead here

**India**

| Course | Length (approximate) | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| MBBS | 4.5 years + 1 year internship | The main degree to become a doctor |
| BDS | 4 years + 1 year internship | Dentistry |
| BAMS, BHMS, BUMS | 4.5 years + 1 year internship | Ayurveda, Homoeopathy, Unani |
| BSc Nursing, BPT, BPharm | 4 years | Allied health careers (not doctors, but in healthcare) |

**Abroad (common choices)**

- **United Kingdom:** a 5 or 6 year MBBS/MBChB straight after Grade 12. Very competitive, needs the UCAT test, an interview, and strong A Level or IB grades. Fees are high (approximately £35,000 to £50,000 a year for international students). See our post on [Studying in the UK](/countries/studying-in-the-uk/).
- **United States:** you cannot do medicine straight after school. You first do a 4 year bachelor's degree, then the MCAT exam, then 4 years of medical school. Very expensive and very hard for international students.
- **Other countries** (Russia, Georgia, Philippines, Kazakhstan and others) offer MBBS to Indian students at lower cost. **Important:** to practise in India afterwards you must clear the Indian licensing exam (currently FMGE, moving to NExT). Check the NMC site for which foreign degrees are recognised before paying any fees.

## Entrance tests

- **NEET (UG)** is the one and only entrance test for MBBS, BDS and AYUSH courses in India, in both government and private colleges. It is held once a year, usually in May, and covers Physics, Chemistry and Biology from Grades 11 and 12.
- Admission is through counselling: 15% of government seats through All India Quota (MCC) and the rest through your state's counselling.
- Approximate figures: over 20 lakh students write NEET each year for roughly 1 lakh MBBS seats. Government seats are far fewer than private ones.

For medicine abroad: **UCAT** (UK), **MCAT** (USA, after a bachelor's degree), plus an English test such as IELTS.

## Skills to start building now

- Read about the body: good popular science books and videos
- Learn to study consistently rather than in bursts. NEET rewards steady work from Grade 11.
- Volunteer or shadow at a clinic or hospital if you get the chance (ask your FP counsellor how)
- Practise explaining ideas simply to friends or family. Doctors must communicate well.

## Where people work and how the career grows

Doctors work in government and private hospitals, clinics, the armed forces, research labs, public health, medical companies and universities. After MBBS the usual path is:

1. Internship (1 year, part of the degree)
2. Postgraduate entrance (currently NEET PG, moving to NExT) for MD or MS (3 years)
3. Super-specialisation (DM or MCh, 3 more years), if you want

Many doctors also move into hospital management, health policy, medical writing or health technology.

## Approximate starting pay in India

Marked approximate; it varies a lot by city, hospital and specialisation.

- Junior resident after MBBS in a government hospital: approximately ₹60,000 to ₹1,00,000 a month
- After MD/MS, in private practice or corporate hospitals: much higher over time
