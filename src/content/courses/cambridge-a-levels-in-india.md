## The short answer

Yes. Cambridge International AS and A Levels count in India. JEE, NEET and CUET all accept them, and so do Indian universities. Cambridge itself says that public universities usually write "any 10+2 equivalent qualification", while many private universities name Cambridge A Level on their admissions pages.

But "accepted" does not always mean "no extra paperwork". Some admissions ask for one or two extra certificates, and almost all of them want your results as **marks**, not grades. This guide explains what to expect, so nothing surprises you in Grade 12.

A few words first:

- **Equivalence certificate:** a paper that says your Cambridge qualification is at the same level as Class 12 of an Indian board.
- **Eligibility certificate:** a paper from one particular university saying you may join its colleges. It is different from equivalence, and only some universities ask for it.
- **PUM (Percentage Uniform Mark):** a mark out of 100 that Cambridge prints next to each grade on your statement of results. Indian universities use it as your "percentage".

## 1. Equivalence: who gives it now

Since April 2025, India's **University Grants Commission (UGC)**, the body that oversees universities, has issued equivalence certificates for school qualifications from foreign boards. Before that, the Association of Indian Universities (AIU) did this job. The rules are the UGC's Equivalence Regulations 2025, published on 4 April 2025.

**Do you need one?** Often, no. Cambridge's own India FAQ says most Indian institutions, including the IITs, do not ask for an equivalence certificate when you took your Cambridge exams in India. The UGC rules are written mainly for schooling done in another country. Ask for one only when a university or exam asks you for it.

**How to apply, if you are asked:**

1. Go to the UGC Equivalence Portal, [equivalence.ugc.ac.in](https://equivalence.ugc.ac.in/), and register.
2. Fill in the form and upload your documents: certificates, statements of results, and details of your school and the course.
3. Pay the fee shown on the portal. (The UGC had not printed the amount on its public pages when I checked.)
4. Track your application on the portal. The rules say the UGC should give its decision within 15 working days of getting a complete application.
5. If you are refused, you can ask for one review within 30 working days.

The portal has a separate School Education help desk: <equivalence@ugc.gov.in>, 011-23604520.

**Where does AIU fit now?** You will still see "AIU equivalence certificate" on some university forms and in the JEE Main bulletin, because many documents have not been updated. AIU's website also still describes its equivalence service. The CUET-UG 2026 bulletin, on the other hand, already says UGC. If a form says AIU, ask the admissions office in writing whether a UGC certificate will do.

## 2. Admissions that want an extra certificate

These are the Tamil Nadu examples I could check on official pages.

**Tamil Nadu MBBS and BDS (state counselling).** The 2026–27 prospectuses of the Tamil Nadu Medical Selection Committee say that students from boards other than the State Board, CBSE and ISC must upload an **Eligibility Certificate from the Tamil Nadu Dr. M.G.R. Medical University** with the online application. Without it, the application is rejected. This applies to Government Quota and to Management Quota seats. You apply for this certificate to the university ([tnmgrmu.ac.in](https://www.tnmgrmu.ac.in/)), so start as soon as your results are out.

**Colleges under the University of Madras.** The University of Madras exempts only the Tamil Nadu Higher Secondary, CBSE and ISC from its rule. Everyone else, including Cambridge students, needs a **Provisional Eligibility Certificate** before joining one of its colleges in Chennai. You apply online, and the fee was about ₹230 in 2026–27 for students who took their exams in India. The University of Madras's own instructions still ask students from abroad for an AIU equivalence certificate.

**Tamil Nadu engineering counselling (TNEA).** The TNEA brochure does not ask for a separate equivalence certificate. It does have a marks rule, explained in section 4 below.

Other universities set their own rules. Some name Cambridge directly, and some ask for nothing extra. Always read that year's prospectus.

## 3. JEE, NEET and CUET for Cambridge students

**JEE Main (NTA).** The JEE Main 2027 bulletin lists "GCE A Level (London/Cambridge/Sri Lanka)" as an accepted qualifying exam, so Cambridge A Level students can sit it. Cambridge's FAQ adds that JEE needs one language: any A Level language, or any AS Level English. IELTS or TOEFL do not count instead.

**JEE Advanced and the IITs.** To get an IIT seat, you must also pass the Class 12 rule. In the JEE Advanced 2026 brochure, that means **at least 75% aggregate** (65% for SC, ST and PwD students) **or a place in the top 20 percentile of your board**, with Physics, Chemistry and Maths. Some details matter for Cambridge students:

- The 75% is worked out over **five subjects**: Physics, Chemistry, Maths, a language and one more subject, each scaled to 100.
- Cambridge does not publish a top-20-percentile cut-off for its students. When a board does not publish one, the brochure says the **CBSE cut-off** is used. For most Cambridge students, the 75% route is the safer one to plan for.
- If your board gives only grades, you must bring a certificate of equivalent marks when you accept a seat. Cambridge students in India get PUMs, which solves this.

NITs and IIITs fill their seats through JoSAA, which has its own Class 12 rule, so check the JoSAA rules for the year too. The JEE Advanced 2027 brochure was not out yet when I checked.

**NEET-UG (for MBBS, BDS and more).** The National Medical Commission (NMC) sets the subjects. You need **Physics, Chemistry, Biology or Biotechnology, and English**, with practical work in the sciences. The NEET-UG 2026 bulletin reminds students from international boards to make sure their last two years of study cover all of these. Cambridge's FAQ advises sitting **AS Level English** in Grade 12, because IELTS or TOEFL cannot replace an English subject. If you plan on medicine, keep AS English in your timetable.

**CUET-UG (for central universities such as Delhi University).** The CUET-UG 2026 bulletin accepts "GCE A Level (London/Cambridge/Sri Lanka)". Boards not on its list need a **UGC** certificate. Each university then applies its own subject and marks rules, so check the programme page of each university you choose.

## 4. How your grades become a percentage

Cambridge students in India get a PUM for every subject. Cambridge's FAQ says Indian universities should use these PUMs and **not convert grades**. To get an aggregate, add up the PUMs of the subjects asked for and divide by how many there are. For A Levels taken in two stages (AS, then A2), use the A Level PUMs, not the AS ones.

A few institutions say on their own pages how they read Cambridge results:

- **Shiv Nadar University (Delhi NCR).** Its 2026 admissions page asks Cambridge students for at least two A Levels and an aggregate of **65% PUM or more**.
- **TNEA (Tamil Nadu engineering).** The brochure says that if you show only grade certificates, you must also give your actual marks. If you don't, only the **lowest mark of each grade band** is counted. Always send your PUMs, or your marks may be counted lower than you actually scored.
- **Tamil Nadu MBBS and BDS, NRI seats.** The 2026–27 Management Quota prospectus says NRI applicants must give the marks behind their grades, or the **minimum marks** will be assumed.

Cambridge results for the June series come out in August, which is late for many Indian admissions. Cambridge suggests that students aiming for Indian universities take the **March series** where they can. In India, March results come out in May.

## 5. The subject choice that matters most

**Dropping Mathematics after IGCSE closes most routes into engineering, data, AI, finance and consulting**, in India and abroad. JEE needs Physics, Chemistry and Maths, and so do the IITs' 75% rule and TNEA. If you are not sure what you want to study, keeping Maths at least to AS Level is the safer choice.

For medicine, keep Biology and Chemistry, plus AS English. The [Career Roadmaps](/roadmaps/) page shows which school subjects keep which doors open for 26 careers.

## A short checklist

- **Grade 10:** choose AS and A Level subjects with your target courses in mind. Keep Maths for engineering and data. Keep Biology, Chemistry and AS English for medicine.
- **Grade 11:** make a list of five places you might apply to. Note which ones ask for an equivalence or eligibility certificate.
- **Grade 12:** keep your IGCSE and AS statements of results, migration certificate and transfer certificate safe. Consider the March exam series.
- **When results come out:** work out your aggregate from your PUMs. Apply at once for any eligibility certificate, such as the Dr. M.G.R. Medical University or University of Madras one.
- **Every year:** read the new bulletin or prospectus. Rules and dates change.

## Questions to ask a university

1. Do you accept Cambridge AS and A Levels for this course? How many A Levels do you need, and which subjects?
2. Do I need an equivalence certificate (UGC) or your own eligibility certificate? When must I submit it?
3. Will you use my Cambridge PUMs as my marks? Which subjects go into the aggregate?
4. Do my AS Level subjects count, or only full A Levels?
5. My results come out in August. Can I still apply, or should I take the March series?
6. Can you confirm all this in writing, by email?
