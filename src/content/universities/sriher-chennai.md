## At a glance

- **University:** Sri Ramachandra Institute of Higher Education and Research (SRIHER). It began in 1985 as Sri Ramachandra Medical College and Research Institute. The Government of India made it a deemed university in September 1994 (a deemed university is one the UGC lets set its own courses and exams). It took its present name in 2018.
- **Location:** Porur, Chennai, Tamil Nadu. The campus has a large teaching hospital and hostels for about 6,000 students.
- **NAAC:** A++ (NAAC is the national body that grades the quality of Indian universities; A++ is its top grade).
- **NIRF 2025:** 21st for Medical, 13th for Dental, 36th for Pharmacy and 60th among universities. NIRF is the Government of India's own ranking. NIRF 2026 was not on nirfindia.org when this was checked, so these are the latest.
- **Faculties:** Medicine, Dentistry, Pharmacy, Nursing, Allied Health Sciences, Physiotherapy, Biomedical Sciences and more, with over 160 programmes.

## Which courses are most popular?

- **MBBS** (250 seats: 212 general and 38 NRI) and **BDS** (100 seats: 85 general and 15 NRI), for 2026-27.
- **Nursing and pharmacy:** B.Sc Nursing (160 seats), B.Pharm (100 seats) and Pharm.D (30 seats).
- **Allied health:** Bachelor of Physiotherapy (BPT, 100 seats), Occupational Therapy, Optometry, Audiology and Speech-Language Pathology, Cardiac Technology and many other B.Sc courses.
- **Life sciences:** B.Sc (Hons) Biomedical Sciences (90 seats), Medical Biotechnology, Medical Microbiology and Clinical Psychology.

## How do you get into MBBS and BDS?

SRIHER does not run its own test for MBBS or BDS. Every seat, general and NRI, is filled through a NEET (UG) rank and counselling by the Medical Counselling Committee (MCC), a central government body. This is the "deemed university" round of MCC counselling, and it is open to students from every state, so a Tamil Nadu student competes with the whole country. It is separate from Tamil Nadu's own state counselling.

**NEET (UG) basics, from the official 2026 bulletin.** The student must be 17 by 31 December of the exam year, and there is no upper age limit. The qualifying Class 12 exam must include Physics, Chemistry, Biology or Biotechnology, and English, with practical work in the sciences. In 2026, applications ran from 8 February to 8 March 2026 and the exam was on 3 May 2026. The fee was ₹1,700 for the General category in India. NEET 2027 had not been announced when this was checked, so watch neet.nta.nic.in.

**MCC deemed university counselling.** In 2026, a student choosing deemed university seats paid a ₹5,000 registration fee (not refunded) and a ₹2,00,000 security deposit (refundable under MCC rules) at registration.

### CBSE and State Board students

Take Physics, Chemistry, Biology and English in Class 11 and 12. Sit NEET, then register with MCC for deemed university seats. SRIHER checks every NMC eligibility rule again when the student reports. A student who does not meet them is not admitted, even with an allotment letter.

### Cambridge AS & A Level students

- **Subjects.** Plan Physics, Chemistry and Biology at A Level, with English. NTA's 2026 bulletin says a student from a foreign or international board must show that the last two years of school covered Physics, Chemistry and Biology (with practical tests) and English to the same scope and standard as an Indian Class 12. SRIHER's own MBBS prospectus asks NRI candidates for Physics, Chemistry, Biology or Biotechnology, and English.
- **Equivalence certificate.** SRIHER's 2026-27 MBBS prospectus asks students with foreign qualifications for an equivalence certificate from the University Grants Commission (UGC). Since 2025, UGC issues these certificates for school results through its portal, equivalence.ugc.ac.in. NTA's NEET bulletin still mentions the Association of Indian Universities (AIU), the body that used to do this, so apply to UGC and keep the certificate ready before counselling.
- **Grade conversion.** SRIHER does not publish a Cambridge grade to percentage table. Cambridge's statement of results shows a percentage uniform mark (PUM) for each AS and A Level subject, and Indian universities often use these. Ask SRIHER which figure it will use.

## How to get in: nursing, pharmacy and allied health

These courses do **not** use NEET. SRIHER runs its own All India Common Entrance Examination, in two groups.

- **Group A** (Pharm.D, B.Pharm, B.ASLP, B.Sc Nursing, Cardiac Technology, Biomedical Sciences and other B.Sc Hons courses): 180 multiple choice questions, 60 each in Physics, Chemistry and Biology, in English. Students without Biology who apply for B.Pharm, Pharm.D or B.ASLP answer 60 Mathematics questions instead.
- **Group B** covers BPT, Occupational Therapy, Optometry and other allied health courses that follow the National Commission for Allied and Healthcare Professions (NCAHP). Most need at least 50 percent in Physics, Chemistry and Biology in Class 12. For a foreign qualification, the Group B prospectus still says it must be found equivalent by the Association of Indian Universities (AIU) and the NCAHP Equivalence Committee (2026-27, as on 18 Oct 2026). Ask admissions whether a UGC equivalence certificate now replaces the AIU part.
- **Cambridge students (Group A):** Physics, Chemistry and Biology at A Level (Mathematics in place of Biology is allowed for B.Pharm and Pharm.D), English at AS Level or five IGCSE passes including English, and an average of grade D in the three sciences. A few courses ask for an average of C, so check the course line in the prospectus.
- **B.Sc Nursing:** the Group A prospectus also asks for 45 percent in Physics, Chemistry and Biology together and a pass in English (40 percent for reserved categories), and a score at or above the 50th percentile in the SRIHER entrance (2026-27, as on 18 Oct 2026).
- **2026 cycle (for reference):** online forms from 11 February to 20 May 2026, application fee ₹1,000 for each group, exam on 31 May 2026 in Chennai, Coimbatore and Madurai. SRIHER says there is no management quota for these courses. The 2027 dates were not out when this was checked.

## How much does it cost a year?

All figures are from SRIHER's own 2026-27 prospectuses and are for the general (non-NRI) category.

- **MBBS:** tuition about ₹35 lakh a year for four years, then ₹17.5 lakh for the last half year. The amount due at admission in 2026-27 was about ₹36.2 lakh. That covers tuition, ₹20,000 of one-time fees, health insurance and a refundable ₹1 lakh caution deposit. Many older websites still show the earlier, lower fee.
- **BDS:** tuition about ₹6 lakh a year.
- **MBBS and BDS hostel:** about ₹1.4 lakh a year for boys and ₹1.6 lakh for girls in MBBS (AC single rooms), plus a refundable ₹50,000 deposit and mess at about ₹70,000 a year.
- **Nursing, pharmacy and allied health tuition:** about ₹1 lakh to ₹3 lakh a year (2026-27, as on 18 Oct 2026). For example, B.Sc Nursing is ₹1.5 lakh, B.Pharm ₹1.75 lakh, and BPT and BOT ₹3 lakh. Hostel fees for these courses were not in the prospectus, so ask the admissions office.

## Are there scholarships?

SRIHER says it offers scholarships for rural and underprivileged students with good marks, and lists in-house and external schemes, but it does not publish the rules or amounts. Ask the admissions office before you count on one. Central and state schemes on the National Scholarship Portal (scholarships.gov.in) and bank education loans also apply.

## Can NRI, OCI and foreign students apply?

- **MBBS and BDS NRI seats** (38 and 15) are also filled through NEET and MCC. The NRI MBBS fee for 2026-27 was US$2,60,000 for the whole course, paid in three parts by wire transfer. NRI-sponsored candidates need the sponsor's passport and visa, proof of the relationship, a notarised affidavit and an Embassy certificate. Foreign nationals must show an OCI card or a student visa at admission.
- **MCC money and unfilled seats:** MCC sends refunds only to an Indian NRO account, not an NRE account. NRI seats left unfilled turn into general seats in Round 3 (MCC 2026 bulletin, as on 18 Oct 2026).
- **Other courses:** NRIs and foreign nationals apply through SRIHER's International Relations Office (admissions.international@sriramachandra.edu.in), with separate NRI and foreign national forms and prospectuses on the admissions page.
- **Rules for nursing, pharmacy and allied health (2026-27 NRI and foreign national prospectuses, as on 18 Oct 2026):** Seats go first come, first served on Class 12 or A Level marks and each course's own eligibility. Foreign nationals include OCI cardholders. NRI candidates are normally exempt from the SRIHER entrance, though an online interview may be held. Choosing the NRI category is final: a student cannot move to the regular category later. Tuition is quoted in US dollars for the whole course and paid in three instalments by wire transfer. Examples: BPT and BOT US$26,500, Pharm.D US$50,000, B.Pharm US$16,500 (NRI) and B.Sc Nursing US$10,500 (foreign national). Hostel is US$450 (non-AC twin) to US$1,700 (AC single) a year, with mess extra. A UGC equivalence certificate is needed, and the university registers foreign nationals with the FRRO.

## What should families know before applying?

- **MBBS here is a national, all-India seat with a very high fee.** Tuition alone is about ₹1.6 crore across the course at 2026-27 rates. Decide what the family can afford before MCC choice filling.
- **A strong backup inside the same campus.** A student who wants a health career but misses MBBS can still sit SRIHER's own test at the end of May for nursing, pharmacy, physiotherapy or a biomedical course. Put that date in the plan early.
- **PCM students have options too.** B.Pharm, Pharm.D and B.ASLP accept Mathematics in place of Biology.
- **Keep every Cambridge subject in the March series.** NEET counselling starts in summer, and SRIHER checks marks at reporting. A subject left for a later series can make a student ineligible on the day.
