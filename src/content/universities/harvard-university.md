## Harvard Admission Requirements, CBSE and Cambridge A Level

Harvard looks at the whole student. Marks matter, but they are only the starting point. Essays, teacher letters, activities and a student's own story count too. No two successful applications look the same. Please reach out to the FP (Future Pathway) team for a one on one counselling session.

| Requirement | CBSE Grade 12 | Cambridge A Level |
|---|---|---|
| Boards Accepted | Harvard says requirements are the same for Indian and US applicants and that it sets no quotas by country or school location. CBSE, ISC and State Board students send their school record in the same way | Cambridge International AS and A Level. Harvard asks the school to send predicted grades, based on classwork and mock exams |
| Minimum to Apply | Harvard publishes no minimum mark. A strong record through Classes 9 to 12 is the starting point | Harvard publishes no minimum. A strong A Level record in three or more subjects is the starting point |

## Common to both pathways

Harvard uses the Common Application (commonapp.org). The application fee is USD 95 (~INR 9,100 at ~USD 96/INR, October 2026). A fee waiver is available if the fee is a hardship. All financial aid families send the CSS Profile. Parents also upload income and tax proof from India. If there is no tax return, Harvard accepts salary certificates or an employer letter that states yearly income. Papers that are not in English need a translation. Harvard says need is judged only after admission. It never affects the admission decision.

Harvard also has a Lakshmi Mittal South Asia Institute, which studies South Asia and runs an India Conference. Please check its website for dates.

## Why Should Your Child Consider Harvard University?

### #5 in QS and #6 in THE, With a 390 Year Record

Harvard was founded in 1636. It is the oldest university in the United States. In the QS World University Rankings 2027 (June 2026) Harvard is #5. In the THE World University Rankings 2027 (30 September 2026) it is #6, down from joint 5th last year. The two lists use different methods, so the numbers differ. Both place Harvard among the best in the world. Harvard graduates are respected by employers and universities around the globe. A degree here opens doors, but it does not decide a career alone.

### Liberal Arts Education, With Time to Explore

Harvard College teaches the liberal arts. Your child can try courses across many fields before choosing a main subject. Harvard calls the main subject a concentration. It offers more than 2,900 courses across 50 concentrations. This suits a student who is strong in several subjects, or who is curious and has not yet settled on one. It can feel less direct than a course such as Engineering or Medicine at a UK university. Please ask the FP team when a student must choose a concentration, because we could not confirm the time on Harvard's pages.

### Need Blind, and Harvard Meets 100 Percent of Need

Harvard is one of six US universities that are need blind for international students and meet full need. The others are Amherst, Dartmouth, MIT, Princeton and Yale. Need blind means your family's money is never looked at when Harvard decides on admission. Full need means Harvard covers the gap between what the family can pay and the cost. For 2026 to 2027, Harvard says families earning under USD 100,000 (~INR 96 lakhs) a year are not expected to pay anything. Families earning up to USD 200,000 (~INR 1.9 crores) get at least the full tuition covered. Above that, aid is worked out for each family. These limits assume typical savings, and they leave out home value and retirement money. Harvard also gives a USD 2,000 start up grant in the first year (~INR 1.9 lakhs). Harvard gives no merit scholarships. All aid is based on need.

### Harvard and Indian Families

Harvard says about 16 percent of the Class of 2029 are international students. Cambridge, Massachusetts is also home to MIT, so the area has a very large student community. We could not confirm a current number of Indian students at Harvard, so please ask the FP team or check Harvard's own pages.

## What parents should know

- CRITICAL. Harvard requires the SAT or ACT, and Restrictive Early Action closes on 1 November 2026, soon after the date of this document. Harvard asks for early action test scores by the end of October. Most families who have not yet sat the test should plan for Regular Decision on 1 January 2027 and book the next test date at once. TOEFL and IELTS cannot take the place of the SAT or ACT.
- CRITICAL. Aid depends on your family income, and you must apply for it. Harvard gives no merit scholarship. It says families under USD 100,000 (~INR 96 lakhs) pay nothing toward attendance. Families up to USD 200,000 (~INR 1.9 crores) have full tuition covered. Families above that may still get aid, but there is no promise. Students are expected to earn about USD 3,500 a year for personal costs, usually through campus work. Loans are available if a family wishes, but Harvard says it will never require them.
- Yearly cost for 2026 to 2027 is USD 91,634 in bills from Harvard (~INR 88 lakhs at ~USD 96/INR, October 2026). That is tuition USD 62,226, fees USD 6,216, housing USD 14,250 and food USD 8,942. Add about USD 3,500 for books and personal costs and up to USD 5,000 for travel. The full total is USD 95,134 to 100,134 (~INR 91–96 lakhs). Health insurance is USD 4,954 more, unless a family plan covers the student. Most families who get aid pay far less.
- Harvard's acceptance rate was 4.2 percent for the Class of 2029 (2,003 admitted from 47,893 applicants), the latest figure Harvard has published. Harvard no longer publishes separate early and regular numbers. Please do not rely on older figures that say early applicants have much better odds. Academic marks are the starting point. Essays, teacher letters and activities do the rest.
- Restrictive Early Action is not binding. It does limit other early applications to private US colleges until Harvard replies in mid December. UK applications through UCAS, Oxford and Cambridge included, are allowed. Non binding early programmes at public US universities are also allowed.
- Check each year: the court case about international students. In May 2025 the US government said it was withdrawing Harvard's SEVP certification, which is the approval needed to enrol international students. Harvard challenged this in court. In June 2025 a federal court issued orders that pause the government's actions. In August 2025 the government said it would not use its May letter to revoke the certification. Harvard's International Office says the orders stay in place while the case goes on. It says Harvard can keep issuing the I-20 form. It also says the court's rulings are not final. A law firm summary reports that the government's appeal is set for a hearing on 6 October 2026. We do not know the outcome, so please check the Harvard International Office page before you decide.
- US visa rules have changed. Since June 2025, F-1 visa applicants must list their social media accounts of the past five years and set them to public. India is not on the US travel ban list. Appointment waits change by month and by city, so check the US State Department page and apply as soon as the I-20 arrives. Budget about USD 185 for the visa fee and USD 350 for the SEVIS fee. A further USD 250 "visa integrity fee" exists in US law. We could not confirm whether Indian consulates charge it yet.
- Stay length and work after study. From 15 September 2026 the US ends "duration of status" for F-1 students. A student is now admitted for the length of the programme, up to a maximum of 4 years, and must apply to extend if more time is needed. A four year Harvard degree fits inside that limit. OPT (work for 12 months after study, and 24 months more for STEM subjects) still exists. The US government says it is reviewing it, and we found no published rule to end it. Harvard has also said it approves CPT, which is work during study, only when an internship is a degree requirement. Harvard College students are therefore generally not eligible. The USD 100,000 fee on new H-1B work visas has been challenged in court and is blocked for now. Rules here are moving fast, so please check again before your child applies.
