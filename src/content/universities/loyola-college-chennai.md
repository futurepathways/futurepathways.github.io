## At a glance

- **What it is:** An autonomous college of the University of Madras. Autonomous means the college sets its own syllabus and exams, while the degree is from the University of Madras. Loyola has been autonomous since 1978, and the UGC extended this for 2022 to 2032. It is run by the Jesuits (the Society of Jesus) and opened in 1925.
- **Where:** Nungambakkam, Chennai, Tamil Nadu.
- **Quality marks:** 14th among colleges in India's NIRF 2025 rankings (8th in 2024). NAAC A++ with a score of 3.55 out of 4 (2021). NIRF 2026 had not been published on 15 November 2026.
- **Two shifts:** Shift I (8.15 am to 1.15 pm) is government aided, with very low fees. Shift II (1.30 pm to 6.30 pm) is self-supporting, with higher fees. A few Shift II courses, such as B.Com (Honours) and the B.Voc courses, run in the morning.
- **Who gets priority:** Loyola says it gives priority to Dalit Catholics, Catholics, tribal communities and other marginalised groups, and preference to students from nearby districts. It welcomes students from other states and countries.

## Which courses are most popular?

Loyola is known for commerce (B.Com General, Honours, Accounting and Finance, Corporate Secretaryship), BBA, BCA, economics, English, visual communication and media, and the sciences (Chemistry, Physics, Mathematics, Statistics, Zoology and Plant Biology).

**Women students:** Most undergraduate courses admit men only. Women can apply for these seven: B.A. French, B.Sc. Visual Communication, BMM (Multimedia and Animation), B.Voc. Digital Journalism, B.Voc. 3D Animation, B.Sc. Advanced Zoology and Biotechnology, and B.Sc. Plant Biology and Plant Biotechnology. All postgraduate courses are open to women.

## How do CBSE and other Indian board students get in?

- **Subjects:** Each course needs certain Class 12 subjects. For example, B.Com needs Commerce or Business Studies, Accountancy, Economics or Computer Science, and Mathematics, Business Maths or Statistics. BCA needs Mathematics, Business Maths, Computer Science or Statistics. Arts courses such as English and History take any group.
- **Minimum marks:** The 2026-27 prospectus does not print a minimum percentage. Competition is strong, so high Class 12 marks matter.
- **Entrance test:** Most courses have none. A screening test is used to shortlist for B.Sc. Visual Communication, BMM, B.Voc, B.Com Accounting and Finance, B.Com (Honours) and B.Com Computer Applications. Loyola does not use CUET for these courses.
- **Selection:** Shortlists go up on the online portal. Original documents are then checked in person on a date the college announces.
- **Eligibility certificate:** CBSE and ISC students do not need one (they are on the University of Madras exempt list, provided they passed at least five subjects including English).

## How do Cambridge AS & A Level students get in?

- **Same subject rules** as above, read against your A Level subjects. Loyola's fee tables have a separate "Others" column for boards other than Tamil Nadu HSC, CBSE and ISC, so other boards are accepted.
- **University of Madras eligibility certificate (verified):** Loyola's 2026-27 prospectus asks for an eligibility certificate from the University of Madras from students of boards other than the Tamil Nadu board. The University of Madras says that only the Tamil Nadu Higher Secondary, CBSE and ISC are exempt, and that students of "all other" boards, in India or abroad, must get a Provisional Eligibility Certificate. Cambridge is not on the exempt list, so a Cambridge student should plan to get this certificate. Cross-check this with the Loyola admissions office and the University of Madras before you apply.
  - **Fee (2026-27):** ₹230 if the qualifying exam was passed in India; ₹5,030 for undergraduate courses if passed outside India. Not refunded.
  - **How:** Apply online on the University of Madras eligibility portal, then send the documents by speed post to the Registrar, University of Madras, Chepauk, Chennai 600 005. The 2026-27 window closed on 30 September 2026, so watch for the 2027-28 window.
- **Grade conversion:** Loyola does not publish a way to turn Cambridge grades into marks. Ask the admissions office how they will enter and compare your A Level results.
- **Equivalence for foreign candidates:** The University of Madras instructions for students who studied outside India still ask for an AIU equivalence certificate. Since April 2025, equivalence for foreign school qualifications is granted by the UGC through its equivalence portal, so ask the University of Madras which certificate it will accept now.
- **Late results:** Loyola says online registration must be done within 10 working days of the Tamil Nadu +2 results. If your board's results are not out by the last date, you must register within 3 working days of your results coming out. Ask the college early how this works for your Cambridge results.

## When do applications open, and what is the fee?

- **When:** Applications open around the Tamil Nadu +2 results, usually in May. The 2027-28 dates had not been announced on 15 November 2026.
- **Fee:** ₹200 per undergraduate application, not refunded. You can apply for any number of courses you are eligible for. SC and ST applicants do not pay for their first three applications.
- **Apply only yourself.** Loyola says it takes no donations for admission, and only the applicant's own mobile number and Gmail address should be used on the form.

## How much does it cost a year?

Loyola publishes the first-semester fee for 2026-27. A year has two semesters; the second-semester fee is not printed, so the yearly figures below are rough estimates that assume a similar fee.

| Example course | First semester fee, 2026-27 ("Others" boards) | Rough yearly tuition |
|---|---|---|
| B.Com (General), Shift I (aided) | about ₹16,175 | about ₹32,000 |
| B.Com (General), Shift II | about ₹55,305 | about ₹1.1 lakh |
| B.Com (Honours), Shift II | about ₹88,335 | about ₹1.8 lakh |
| B.Sc. Visual Communication, Shift II | about ₹62,825 | about ₹1.3 lakh |

**Hostel, 2026-27:** Admission to the college does not guarantee a hostel place. In the men's hostel, the A Block costs about ₹1.3 lakh a year (non-AC) to ₹2.2 lakh (AC with attached bathroom), and the LICET Block about ₹1.2 lakh; these include mess fees till April 2027. The common blocks cost about ₹30,000 plus a monthly mess bill. The women's hostel asks for an initial payment of ₹1,10,000 for 2026-27. Its full fee for 10 months with food is ₹1,11,000 to ₹1,45,000 for a non-AC room and ₹1,81,000 to ₹2,26,000 for an AC room (2026-27 women's hostel prospectus, as on 18 Oct 2026). Hostel admission is for one year at a time.

## Are there scholarships?

The 2026-27 prospectus does not print a merit scholarship table. Students can apply for central and Tamil Nadu government scholarships for which they qualify. The women's hostel offers financial support to socially disadvantaged hostellers. For other college support, ask the admissions office.

The college's Students Support Services annual report for 2023-24 (as on 18 Oct 2026) names its own support for families with limited means:

- **Jesuit Education Support (JES):** full and partial support for students from poor backgrounds, chosen in the first semester. There were 867 partial awards in 2023-24.
- **Rector Scholarship:** full support for a small number of students.
- **Alumni schemes and endowments:** such as Help A Student. Amounts vary.
- **Corporate scholarships:** such as Sumitomo and RKMF, for poor but meritorious students.
- **Government schemes:** for SC and ST, BC and MBC, first-graduate, differently abled and rural girl students. The college helps students apply.

## Can NRI, OCI and foreign students apply?

There is no separate NRI or foreign quota or fee on the official pages. Students from other countries use the same online form and upload their passport and visa. A student who studied outside India needs the University of Madras eligibility certificate through the "passed outside India" route (₹5,030 for undergraduate courses in 2026-27).

## What should families know before applying?

- **Check gender first.** Most undergraduate courses, including B.Com, BBA, BCA, Economics, Mathematics, Physics and Computer Science, admit men only.
- **Cambridge students: start the eligibility certificate early.** Keep your A Level and IGCSE certificates and transfer certificate ready.
- **Shift matters.** Shift I fees are very low, but places are few. Many students from outside Chennai end up in Shift II.
- **Hostel places are limited** and must be applied for separately.
- **No donations.** Report anyone who asks for money for a seat to the college.
